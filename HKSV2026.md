# 4K HomeKit Secure Video

Pylo can record native 3840×2160 HEVC at 30 fps through its existing encrypted
HomeKit Data Stream (HDS) transport. Enable **4K Secure Video Recording** in
Pylo’s camera settings, then use **Stream and Record** in Home.
The option defaults off and requires iOS 17.4+/macOS 14+ and a native 4K camera.
Unsupported cameras keep the existing recording path; video is never upscaled
and advertised as native 4K.

## Implementation

The idle capture owner selects a native 4K preset and HEVC Main encoding.
During live viewing, the same native camera input feeds a separate HEVC
recording encoder while the H.264 preview retains its negotiated resolution.
The existing AAC-ELD microphone encoder also feeds recording while live viewing;
Only the implemented 16kHz mono AAC-ELD format is advertised, and the shared
encoder uses 16 kbps to match the tested hub's selection. Home's recording-audio
switch gates those frames. Both tracks use the video clock, preserving their
alignment when capture handoffs drop audio samples. Disabling audio discards
buffered microphone samples, and disarming recording leaves live audio running.
Recording-enable writes preserve prepared stream sessions until Home starts
them; a new recording-enable transition only restarts an already running
capture. Home’s camera-off control stops capture and refuses cached snapshots. Disarming
recording stops the recording encoder; standalone sensors can retain their
capture without encoding recording media.

The MP4 writer emits `hvc1`/`hvcC` using the hardware encoder’s parameter sets.
HEVC fragments include producer reference time (`prft`, version 1, zero flags).
Sample timing rounds timestamp positions, preserving real elapsed time at
15, 24, and 30 fps. A four-second prebuffer sends one complete decodable fragment.

This is the minimal existing-transport recording implementation. It does not
implement the complete HKSV 2026 service profile, CMAF ingestion, WebRTC,
SFrame, or multi-tier RTP. No new dependency is required.

The tested hub selected the legacy 1920×1080 H.264 recording configuration,
while Home preserved the transmitted 3840×2160 HEVC media. This is an
interoperability result, rather than proof of new-protocol capability negotiation.
[Scrypted’s recording implementation](https://github.com/koush/scrypted/blob/main/plugins/homekit/src/types/camera/camera-recording.ts)
provides the reference for HEVC producer timestamps over HDS.

## Device validation

On 2026-10-12, an iPad Pro M4 on iPadOS 27.0.1 captured a real desk scene.
A temporary moving marker and bounded motion event made the clip reproducible
without requiring someone to move in front of the camera. Home saved the clip;
its **Share Clip** export was inspected independently with FFprobe:

| Export | Video | Frame rate | Duration | Audio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 17:00:32 | `hvc1` HEVC Main, 3840×2160 | 30 fps | 28.000 s | Silent AAC-ELD diagnostic track |
| 17:10:39 | `hvc1` HEVC, 3840×2160 | 30 fps | 28.000 s | None |

The first recording was received by the OS 27 HomePod mini. The video-only
recording was received from the first-generation Apple TV’s IP on tvOS 26.6.
The second result established that a dummy audio track is unnecessary.
The reduced build’s normal 4K preference also produced a saved HomePod clip at
17:52:14: HEVC Main `hvc1`, 3840×2160, 29.687 s, no audio. The preference survived
a subsequent launch without arguments. Remaining diagnostic flags and media
caching have been removed from the source.

After installing the final build and removing every diagnostic flag and media
cache, a physical hand-wave triggered a saved event at 20:58. Home’s **Share
Clips** exports were independently inspected: 20:58:17 and 20:58:57 both contain
`hvc1` HEVC Main at 3840×2160 and 30 fps, with durations of 16.000 s and 12.000 s.
Both are video-only; no synthetic motion or moving marker was used. Home groups
these files into an event, so its displayed event duration differs from the
individual exported segment durations.

Stop Device Hub screen sharing before testing camera capture. Screen sharing
produced black frames on the test iPad; stopping it restored camera frames.

Recordings took time to appear in Home; HDS acknowledgements or close reasons
alone did not establish whether a clip had been saved.

With the microphone enabled, physical wave-and-clap tests also produced saved
Home clips. The 22:10:40 export recorded during live viewing contains 24.000 s
of native 3840×2160 HEVC Main at 30 fps and mono 16kHz AAC-ELD. The 22:15:56
idle-recording export contains 20.000 s of the same video and 20.010 s of audio
at approximately 15.8 kbps after matching the hub's requested audio bitrate.
Both exports start both tracks at zero. Native AVAssetReader decoded real
microphone samples with peaks above 0.85; the idle export also passed full video
decoding. These results validate audio recording during idle capture and live
viewing. The earlier live-view test used the previous 24 kbps encoder setting.

Private footage, device identifiers, signing credentials, and local test
preferences are excluded from the repository. A clean Release build on an iPhone 18 Pro Max running iOS 27.0.1 was also
re-paired and tested with a physical wave and claps. Its 22:32:15 Home export
contains native 3840×2160 HEVC Main at 30 fps and mono 16kHz AAC-ELD at about
16 kbps. Full video decoding passed (480 frames, 16.000 s of video samples),
and native audio decoding recovered 255,584 PCM samples with a peak of 0.71.
Home displays 16 s; FFprobe reports an absolute 20 s timeline with video
starting at 4.000 s and audio at 3.990 s. The exported sample duration is the
useful playback duration, rather than the timeline endpoint. The iPhone's
4K preference persisted and Mac Home live viewing was verified. An iPhone 15
was not available for physical testing. The original idle audio capture path is retained.

## Checks

```sh
swift test --package-path Packages/FragmentedMP4
swift test --package-path Packages/HAP
swift test --package-path Packages/Streaming
./scripts/lint.sh
```

Every package passed in Release: 50 FragmentedMP4, 187 HAP, 13 Locked,
27 SRP, 45 SRTP, 31 Sensors, 30 Streaming, and 52 TLV8 tests (435 total).
All 99 app tests (100 parameterized executions per device) passed on macOS
27.0.1, the physical iPad, and the iPhone 18 Pro Max after the final audio changes. Strict formatting
and the clean signed iPad Release build passed. The prepared-stream regression
covers recording enable and repeated writes with 4K both off and on; audio
regressions cover capture handoff, disarming, muted samples, codec bitrate,
and alignment after dropped audio packets.

The clean Release build is installed on the paired iPad. Live preview recovered
after closing competing viewers. Home's Off mode stopped capture; Stream mode
disarmed recording while preserving live viewing. Stream and Record was restored.
Home's Record Audio switch propagated both off and on to Pylo; it is enabled at
the end of validation. No usable iOS simulator runtime was installed, so device
app tests ran on physical hardware instead.
